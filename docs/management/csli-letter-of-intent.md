## 2. Mission Summary

Luna Ice Mapper is a 6U CubeSat scientific mission to map and characterize water-ice deposits in the lunar south polar region, using a neutron spectrometer (NS) and a near-infrared (NIR) hyperspectral camera. The spacecraft is designed for insertion into a ~100 km circular polar lunar orbit (≥85° inclination) to achieve ≥80% coverage of permanently shadowed regions (PSRs) below 80°S latitude.

**Mission statement:** To map and characterize water-ice deposits in the lunar south polar region using a 6U CubeSat equipped with a neutron spectrometer and near-infrared camera, providing decision-grade data for future Artemis landing site selection and In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) initiatives.

## 3. Alignment with NASA Artemis Objectives

Luna Ice Mapper directly supports NASA's Artemis campaign and its In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) goals:

- **Landing site selection** — high-resolution neutron and NIR ice-abundance maps of south polar PSRs directly inform candidate Artemis landing site down-selection.
- **ISRU resource assessment** — quantitative water-ice abundance mapping supports feasibility studies for future in-situ water extraction and propellant production.
- **Open science data** — all mission data will be archived in NASA's Planetary Data System (PDS4) format, ensuring accessibility to the broader Artemis science and engineering community.
- **International partnership** — the mission establishes Indonesia as a contributing partner in lunar exploration, consistent with NASA's approach of broadening international engagement in Artemis.

| **Flight software** | NASA cFS (core Flight System) framework |
| **Primary mission duration** | 6–12 months (extendable to 24 months) |

## 5. Launch Vehicle & Trajectory Needs

Luna Ice Mapper requires a launch opportunity providing (or enabling, via a lunar lander/rideshare secondary payload path) Trans-Lunar Injection (TLI), targeting a Q4 2027 – Q2 2028 launch window compatible with a Ballistic Lunar Transfer (BLT) or low-energy direct transfer trajectory. A detailed launch-provider survey (NASA CSLI/CLPS as primary route, with Rocket Lab Photon and CLPS commercial lander rideshare as backups) has been completed by the mission's launch operations team and is available on request.
